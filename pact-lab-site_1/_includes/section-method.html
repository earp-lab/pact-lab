Our method

Turning involvement into justifiable policy and translation

PACT does more than involve patients and the public; it uses that involvement to produce defensible policy and translation recommendations. It applies the Collective Reflective Equilibrium in Practice (CREP) methodology, developed at CBmE, to take research teams from primary findings to recommendations that can be justified to the public they affect.

The CREP process

CREP is a method, developed at CBmE, for integrating empirical public preference data with normative ethical frameworks to produce policy recommendations that are transparent, reproducible, and publicly justifiable. It is organised as a staged process: assessing when public input is warranted, whose preferences are relevant, what to ask, how to integrate preferences with normative frameworks, and how to justify the final recommendation. The aim is not to read policy off opinion, nor to settle moral truth, but to reach defensible recommendations that the public they affect can be given reasons to accept.

The Health Opinion Panel Singapore (HOPS)

PACT draws on HOPS, a nationally representative public panel of over 4,000 members maintained at CBmE, as a standing resource for gathering public input at scale. HOPS lets research teams obtain structured public attitudes quickly, which then feed the CREP process and the Lab's wider consultation work.

PACT’s methods in practice. In 2026, PACT convened former participants of Project RESET through the Health Opinion Panel Singapore to co-design a large-scale hypertension study, working with the research team to shape trial design before submission. The consultation produced detailed, actionable recommendations on study design, monitoring, and participant support.
