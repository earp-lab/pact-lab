Now

Available to research teams at NUS Medicine and NUHS now, within the capacity of the Lab's two directors while recruitment is underway.

Advice

Planning and costing involvement

Discussion of what involvement a study needs, at what stage, and how to write and cost it into a grant application before submission.

Review

Review of participant-facing materials

Comment on consent forms, information sheets, and recruitment materials, within the capacity of the Lab's founding directors until core staff are in post.

Mapping

Signposting to existing panels

Directing teams to patient, caregiver, and public panels that already exist across the two institutions, so that groups are not created unnecessarily.

Public input

Limited access to HOPS

A small number of public attitude studies each year through the Health Opinion Panel Singapore, subject to panel capacity and to the number of studies panel members take part in each year.

By the end of 2027

Contingent on the two core posts being filled early in 2027.

Mapping

A current map of involvement across NUS Medicine and NUHS

Who convenes which panels, what they cover, who decides on access, and what capacity each has.

Guidance

Standards and standard operating procedures

Written guidance on conducting involvement responsibly and consistently, including reimbursement, recruitment, and documentation.

Public input

A trained public reviewer panel

A subset of HOPS members trained to appraise research from a public perspective, for projects that need considered public judgement rather than survey responses.

Consultation

Facilitated consultation panels

Convening and facilitation of consultation and co-design sessions for a limited number of projects each year, prioritised by fit and readiness.

By the end of 2028

Contingent on the training post and on the access agreements being in place.

Training

Certification for researchers

Training and certification so that research teams can conduct their own involvement to an agreed standard, funded from their own grants. This is what allows the Lab's support to reach more teams than it could serve directly.

Training

The patient and public pathway

Tiered training for patients, caregivers, and members of the public: self-advocacy and navigating the health system, then representing others with the same condition, then formal advocacy roles.

Access

Brokered access to condition-specific groups

Published routes to patient groups held by clinical teams and partner organisations, with agreed criteria, capacity, and points of contact.

Evaluation

Evaluation of involvement and its outcomes

Documentation and evaluation of what involvement changed in the studies that used it, feeding the Lab's research programme.

2029 and beyond

By the end of the second year PACT should no longer be the only route to involvement across NUS Medicine and NUHS. If the training works, involvement becomes something research teams do as a matter of course, to a standard they were taught, with panels and partners they know how to reach. The Lab's role then shifts from providing involvement to setting standards, holding the agreements that make access possible, training each new cohort, and studying what involvement achieves and where it does not.

The wider ambition is a research culture in which patients, caregivers, and members of the public are not consulted at the end but present from the start, and in which the question of what research is worth doing is answered partly by the people it is done for. That takes longer than two years and more than one lab, which is why the training, the standards, and the evidence base matter more than the services.

Requesting support

Approach the Lab as early as possible. Involvement shapes a study most usefully before the design is fixed, and it can only be costed into a grant application if it is planned before submission. Charges apply to some services; the schedule will be published here once it is set.
