The problem it addresses

Public input does not settle policy questions on its own. A majority view may rest on a mistaken premise, may conflict with a commitment the same respondents hold, or may not survive the respondents' own reflection. Nor can normative argument proceed as though public views were irrelevant, since the people affected are owed reasons they can accept.

CREP is a staged process for integrating the two: it treats considered public judgements as evidence to be weighed against theoretical commitments, and revises both until they cohere. The aim is neither to read policy off opinion nor to settle moral truth, but to reach recommendations that are transparent, reproducible, and publicly justifiable.

One method among several

CREP was developed at CBmE and is one of the methods the PACT Lab applies when a project needs to move from public input to a defensible recommendation. It is not the only method the Lab uses, and much PPIE work does not call for it: for example, reviewing participant materials, co-designing a study with the people it will recruit, or convening a condition-specific consultation each require different methods, drawn from the wider literature on patient and public involvement.
