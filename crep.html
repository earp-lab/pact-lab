The problem it addresses

Public input does not settle policy questions on its own. A majority view may rest on a mistaken premise, may conflict with a commitment the same respondents hold, or may not survive the respondents' own reflection. Nor can normative argument proceed as though public views were irrelevant, since the people affected are owed reasons they can accept.

CREP is a staged process for holding both: it treats considered public judgements as evidence to be weighed against theoretical commitments, and revises both until they cohere. The aim is neither to read policy off opinion nor to settle moral truth, but to reach recommendations that are transparent, reproducible, and publicly justifiable.

The stages

Assessing when public input is warranted; establishing whose views are relevant; determining what to ask and how; integrating the results with normative frameworks; and justifying the resulting recommendation in terms the affected public can be given reasons to accept.

One method among several

CREP was developed at CBmE and is the method PACT applies when a project needs to move from public input to a defensible recommendation. It is not the only method the Lab uses, and much involvement work does not call for it: reviewing participant materials, co-designing a study with the people it will recruit, or convening a condition-specific consultation each require different methods, drawn from the wider literature on patient and public involvement.
